About

I left Johns Hopkins in 2024 after eight years there, first as a political theory PhD student and then as a postdoctoral fellow. Since leaving, I have worked in AI evaluation and model-behavior research, including benchmark design, quality review, and empirical analysis.

That work has given me a more technical view of problems I had already encountered in universities: how to set standards for difficult judgment calls, how to make review processes more consistent, and how to use data without pretending that every important decision can be reduced to a metric.

I am interested in bringing that experience back into higher education, particularly in academic programs, assessment, graduate education, admissions, and work around AI in universities. My current technical work is collected on my AI and research portfolio.

I also write philosophical reflections, write about AI, language, mathematics, and politics, and enjoy running in Prospect Park.
